GPS loss

The subsystem keeps publishing a pose during GPS loss.

  • GPS gives a global position while it is available.
  • Without GPS, wheel odometry and the IMU track movement. Their errors accumulate and the estimate drifts.
  • Covariance (the uncertainty value) grows as the estimate drifts.
  • Drone corrections reduce the drift.

Inputs and output

A 2D EKF combines UGV GPS, wheel speed, IMU heading, and optional UAV position corrections.

Drone corrections

The drone is a second position source when the robot's GPS is unusable.

AssumptionDrone localization stays reliable during robot GPS loss.
MeasurementNoisy robot position relative to the drone.
EffectReduces drift and uncertainty.
HardwareSimulation supplies a full 3D vector. A calibrated camera or tag detector must match this contract; range alone is insufficient.

Subsystem connections

Implemented connection: the fused UGV pose and traversability map share the map frame. The planner consumes the fused pose with freshness and uncertainty checks.

Package: cooperative_localization. The fused pose includes optional UAV corrections; the planner connection remains open.

Pose interface

The EKF publishes this fused pose topic. The planner uses this pose with freshness and uncertainty gates.

Consumers

  • Planner: connects to this pose after accuracy and GPS-loss tests.
  • Mapping: publishes traversability data in the same ENU map frame.
  • Coordinates: pose, obstacles, routes, and goals share one frame.
  • Covariance: the estimator's uncertainty in the pose.
  • Frames: the PX4-format anchor is converted from NED/FRD to map ENU/FLU.

Evaluation

Same route under three GPS conditions. Measures estimator error and path-following error.

Test cases

  1. Nominal GPS Regular global position updates.
  2. GPS loss without drone assistance Odometry and IMU only. The estimate drifts.
  3. GPS loss with drone assistance Drone observations correct the estimate.

Metrics

  • Position RMSE: overall position error.
  • Mean and max position error.
  • Heading error.
  • Covariance: reported uncertainty versus actual error.
  • Path-following error.

Scope

In scope

  • UGV pose estimation
  • GPS degradation behavior
  • Odometry and IMU fusion
  • Drone relative-position corrections
  • Pose covariance
  • Planner-facing pose output

Not in scope

  • Terrain classification
  • Path generation
  • Drone path planning
  • Full SLAM (mapping and localizing at once)
  • Loss of localization for both vehicles
  • Commitment to UWB or camera hardware

Assumes the drone has reliable localization while the robot's GPS is degraded. Localization when both vehicles lose their global reference is out of scope.
