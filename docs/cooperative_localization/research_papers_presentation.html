Evaluation of Cooperative Navigation Strategies with Maneuvering UAVs

Pryor, Auburn University master's thesis, 2021 · Paper link

Summary

  • Vehicles that lose GPS share relative measurements to slow their position drift.
  • Compares one central filter with one filter per vehicle.
  • Field test: relative error fell from over 100 m to under 1 m. Absolute error fell from over 70 m to meter level.
  • If one vehicle has an accurate position, the others inherit it once relative positions are accurate.
  • Recommends the central filter for small groups. Its uncertainty values stayed honest.

Relevance

To localization

  • Supports our main claim: a UAV with a reliable position cuts UGV drift during GPS loss.
  • Supports one central estimator and one pose output.
  • Results by measurement type model our three test cases.

To the project

  • Source for the claim that cooperative navigation reduces error growth.
  • UAV flight patterns change accuracy, which links scouting to localization.

Cooperative Localization for GNSS-Denied Subterranean Navigation

Akhihiero, Olawoye, Das, and Gross, NAVIGATION, 2024 · Paper link

Summary

  • A ground robot maps a GPS-denied space and tracks a light drone.
  • The drone pose comes from four inputs at different rates: inertial sensor, altimeter, lidar, and a range radio.
  • Each input has its own assumed noise, for example 0.1 m for the altimeter and range radio.
  • An EKF and a heavier factor-graph method were compared. With all inputs, both were similar, and the EKF was called more practical.
  • Ground robot pose error carried into the drone estimate.

Relevance

To localization

  • Same pattern as ours: several measurements, different rates, one EKF.
  • Gives example noise values for our simulated sensors.
  • UAV pose error will spread into our UGV estimate, so we should model it.

To the project

  • Ground robot tracks drone, the reverse of our case. Cite for method only.
  • The authors list UAV-helps-UGV as future work, which is our subsystem.

UAV-UGV Collaborative Localisation with Minimum Sensing

De Silva and Katupitiya, Sensors, 2024 · Paper link

Summary

  • Two GPS-equipped drones locate a ground vehicle with no GPS, using range readings to radio tags on the vehicle.
  • With only two drones, some positions give a mirror-image wrong answer.
  • A constrained EKF rejects answers the vehicle could not physically reach.
  • Field test: position error under 14 cm when still and about 23 cm when moving. Heading within about 4 degrees.

Relevance

To localization

  • Closest match to our problem statement.
  • Supports rejecting measurements that conflict with how the vehicle can move.
  • Shows why measurement geometry matters if we switch to range-only sensing.

To the project

  • Names search and rescue and disaster use, which matches our goal.
  • Fewer drones means less battery use, which ties to our battery limit.

Fly, Track, Land: Infrastructure-less Magnetic Localization for Heterogeneous UAV-UGV Teaming

Brunacci et al., arXiv preprint, 2026 · Paper link

Summary

  • A small drone lands on a moving ground robot without GPS or outside cameras.
  • The robot emits a magnetic field. The drone reads it to get its position relative to the robot.
  • That reading enters the drone's EKF as a position measurement with tuned noise. Jumps above a threshold are rejected.
  • About 5 cm error hovering and 8 to 11 cm while the robot moves.

Relevance

To localization

  • Model for treating a UAV-UGV relative position as a timestamped measurement with its own noise.
  • Simple outlier check before fusing.
  • Gives accuracy ranges for our sim noise: GNSS 1 to 3 m, UWB 10 to 30 cm.
  • Frame and attitude mistakes show up as position error. Watch our frame contract.

To the project

  • Confirms UAV-UGV teaming is active research.
  • Preprint focused on docking. Use for filter design, not accuracy claims.

A Generalized Extended Kalman Filter Implementation for the Robot Operating System

Moore and Stouch, IAS-13, 2014. Software: robot_localization · Documentation

Summary

  • A ROS package with an EKF and an unscented Kalman filter.
  • Takes any number of sensors as odometry, inertial, or pose and velocity messages with covariance.
  • Keeps estimating from a motion model when data stops. A timeout marks a sensor as lost.
  • Per-sensor outlier rejection thresholds.
  • Publishes its result as Odometry.

Relevance

To localization

  • The planned tool in our technical plan.
  • Continuous estimation matches our rule that the pose never stops publishing.
  • Our contract is PoseWithCovarianceStamped, so we need a small relay node.

To the project

  • A standard ROS tool lowers integration risk.
  • We read the package documentation, not the paper. Read the paper before citing it.

Design choices and gaps

Design choices

  • Build the estimator with robot_localization.
  • Reject stale and out-of-range measurements before fusing.
  • Model UAV pose error, not only UGV sensor noise.
  • Score three cases: nominal GPS, GPS loss without UAV, and GPS loss with UAV.
  • Report uncertainty next to observed error.

Gaps

  • No source yet for Gazebo or PX4 simulation validity.
  • The papers used real sensors or MATLAB. Ours is simulation only, so claims apply to the simulated conditions.
